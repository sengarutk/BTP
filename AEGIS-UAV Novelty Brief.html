AEGIS-UAV · contribution and novelty, 26 Oct 2026

Priced, freshness-aware offloading for a drone camera, measured on a real mesh

What the project contributes, what the measurements show, how it compares with the closest published work, and what a reviewer will ask for.

Evidence: about 1,150 live hardware runs (Raspberry Pi 5 camera drones, Jetson Orin Nano edge drone, laptop ground station; Wi-Fi and a batman-adv mesh), VisDrone video, standard streaming accuracy (sAP). Full numbers: the AEGIS-UAV Results Brief. Updated 30 Oct 2026: a published scheduler re-implemented (AccuMO-style, Results Brief 19.10–19.11).

Verdict

Novel enough for a solid systems-and-measurement paper at a UAV or edge-computing workshop or an IEEE networking conference: no published work we found combines drone offloading over a mesh with pricing of the camera drone's measured energy and heat (with freshness), measured on hardware and scored with streaming accuracy. Freshness × accuracy alone (AoPI) and streaming perception on UAV video alone (StreamFlow) are published, so the claim must stay this narrow.

Not yet enough for a top venue (MobiCom, MobiSys, SenSys): that would need two or more camera drones on the mesh, a published scheduler (AoPI- or AccuMO-style) re-implemented on the testbed (an AccuMO-style planner now runs in the trace replay and in the board code; its board campaign is scripted but not yet run), and ideally a flight or outdoor test. A learned feature-compression baseline is now measured (30 Oct; Results Brief 19.12).

The problem

A small camera drone (Raspberry Pi 5) must detect objects in its video. For every frame it chooses where to run detection: on the Pi itself (slow, always available), on an edge drone with a GPU (Jetson), or on a ground station (laptop GPU), and at what model size and image quality. Results go stale as objects move, so the target is streaming accuracy: the accuracy of the newest result held at every moment, not the accuracy of each result alone. The drone's battery and temperature limit how much it can compute itself.

What was built

  1. A priced controller (v4.1). Per frame it picks the place, model and JPEG quality that maximise expected accuracy after the delay, minus prices. Three prices adapt online by dual ascent: camera-drone energy (power budget 2.8 W), freshness (a result younger than 300 ms at least 95 % of the time), and optionally heat or the edge drone's energy.
  2. A motion forecaster that moves stale boxes to where the objects probably are now, tuned on held-out training clips.
  3. A three-tier testbed: Pi 5 camera drones, a Jetson Orin Nano edge drone and a laptop ground station, on shared Wi-Fi and on an ad-hoc batman-adv mesh (AegisSwarm) with link impairments, forced multi-hop routing and live power, temperature and clock logging.
  4. Detectors fine-tuned on VisDrone (YOLOv8 n/s/m/l, YOLO11 n/s/m), and every earlier campaign re-scored with them.

The dual-ascent pricing itself is a standard technique; the contribution is what is priced together, the objective it optimises (accuracy after delay) and the measurement on hardware. A full literature search is still needed before submission.

Re-implemented baseline (29–30 Oct). AccuMO plans offloading over a 30-frame horizon to maximise predicted accuracy after delay, with content-dependent decay and no energy or heat price. An AccuMO-style planner (252 plans per frame, our measured decay per camera-motion tertile) was run against v4.1 in the trace replay on the same link draws: it ties v4.1 on the mission and good links and is ahead on random links (+0.0044 sAP, 15 seeds; +0.0045 on 10 fresh seeds with the corrected power model), but a LEAF-style fixed energy weight is ahead by as much, and the replay is known to favour such low-onboard policies by about 0.005 sAP against hardware. So the paper must not claim that v4.1 beats planning; what it can claim is that a greedy priced choice does as well as a 30-frame model-predictive planner on this system, at a fraction of the computation (the planner takes 0.6 ms per frame on an idle laptop, 1.9 ms in a live loopback run; not yet timed on the Pi). The planner runs in the board code (adaptive2.py --policy accumo, same decisions as the replay's) and a 45-run mesh campaign against v4.1 and LEAF is scripted; until it runs, this comparison is replay-only.

Revised contribution statement, in the form a strict reviewer would accept: AEGIS-UAV is a real-system study of real-time aerial object-detection offloading across a Raspberry Pi 5 camera drone, a Jetson Orin Nano edge drone and a ground GPU, over Wi-Fi and a batman-adv mesh, scored with streaming AP on VisDrone video. It shows that a controller pricing the camera drone's measured energy and heat, together with detection freshness, keeps or raises streaming accuracy while removing thermal throttling; that the scheduler's objective must use the post-forecasting staleness curve; that the better offloading architecture depends on the mesh topology; and that, for the tested exact splits and encodings, full-frame image offloading beats layer splitting.

Venue fit, revised. Now: IEEE Internet of Things Journal, ACM TECS, UAV and edge workshops (DroNet), IEEE ICC/Globecom/WoWMoM; IEEE TMC with a sharper comparative story. Top tier (MobiCom, MobiSys, SenSys, INFOCOM) needs a learned feature-compression baseline, a multi-drone mesh campaign, a published scheduler (AoPI or AccuMO-style) re-implemented on the testbed, and ideally a flight or outdoor test.

What a reviewer will ask

  • No flight: replayed video, boards on a table one hop apart.
  • One camera drone on the mesh; multi-drone results are simulated.
  • 3 repetitions per mesh cell; some controller variants differ only slightly.
  • A published scheduler (AccuMO-style) is re-implemented, but compared only in the trace replay so far; the board campaign is scripted, not run.
  • The laptop joins the mesh through an SSH tunnel on the ground radio.

What would strengthen it most

  • Two camera drones on the mesh (third Pi): measured contention for the Jetson.
  • A short outdoor or tethered flight with real distances.
  • The AccuMO-style planner on the mesh against v4.1 and LEAF (ported and tested; campaign_mesh4.sh, 45 runs, about 6 h).
  • 5 repetitions in the headline mesh comparisons.
  • A learned feature-compression baseline for the split-versus-JPEG result. Done 30 Oct: 23–76 % fewer bytes than JPEG for the same accuracy, but on the Pi better end to end only at the lowest goodputs.
